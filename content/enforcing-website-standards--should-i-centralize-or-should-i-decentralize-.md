A fully centralized standards enforcement model where one web team owns every page, every edit, every publish protects brand and quality, but it doesn't scale. As one analysis of university website governance puts it, a purely centralized model _puts a small web team in charge of every update, which protects brand and accessibility but creates bottlenecks when the registrar needs to publish a deadline change at 4:55 p.m. on a Friday_ ([Modern Campus](https://moderncampus.com/blog/website-governance-best-practices.html "This link leaves the DubBlog website.")). Every request, however small, has to pass through the same narrow channel.

A fully decentralized model solves the speed problem but creates another. The same source notes that _a purely decentralized model gives every department the keys, which speeds things up but lets brand consistency, accessibility and content quality slip in dozens of small ways across hundreds of pages_ ([Modern Campus](https://moderncampus.com/blog/website-governance-best-practices.html "This link leaves the DubBlog website.")).

Left unchecked, this can result in conflicting information across departments, an inconsistent visual identity from page to page, and the loss of valuable institutional knowledge when experienced people leave.

A more scalable approach blends **centralized standards, decentralized ownership, and automated enforcement.** This approach empowers people with flexibility to manage their own digital properties while maintaining a consistent, compliant, on-brand experience across the organization.

**What’s Included in the Centralized Standards Foundation?**

-   Brand identity, visual design system, and component library.
-   Information architecture and navigation structure.
-   Accessibility standards and legal / compliance requirements.
-   Security, permissions architecture, and technical infrastructure.
-   SEO fundamentals such as URL structure and metadata standards.

**What Gets Allocated to the Organizational Teams?**

-   Day-to-day content creation and updates within approved templates.
-   Page-level copy, imagery, and localized messaging.
-   Publishing cadence for their own section of the site.
-   Minor layout choices within pre-approved component options.

This approach helps turn _standards enforcement_ from a people challenge into a systems-and-governance challenge with three primary objectives:

**Centralized Governance**

Define the organization's baseline for accessibility, content quality, brand, SEO, security, and other standards.

-   Eliminate Ambiguity  
    Create clear, specific standards paired with real-world examples of how to apply them correctly and what _good_ looks like.
-   Make it Actionable  
    Translate standards into practical, task-based guidance that fits naturally into current workflows.  
      
    
-   Treat standards as active and evolving  
    Make standards an ongoing initiative, supported with regular communication, training, updates, and reinforcement.  
      
    
-   Clarify ownership  
    Set clear accountability across central and decentralized groups. Everyone should have a role, but specific individuals are ultimately responsible for the outcome.  
      
    
-   Work with What Exists  
    Design governance around your organization's current structure, tools, and workflows.  
      
    
-   Write for Everyone  
    Use plain language that content editors, marketers, developers, designers, and subject-matter experts can understand.  
      
    
-   Set a Baseline, Then Let People Build On It  
    Move from top-down control to shared organizational standards, allowing groups to add requirements that reflect their specific needs.

**Team Level Ownership**

Give individual groups [ownership and responsibility](people-the-foundation-of-web-governance.html) for applying those standards to their websites and content, and identify _specific individuals_ _ultimately responsible for the outcome_.

-   Pair Accountability with the Required Skills and Tools  
    Give groups the training, templates, tools, and support they need to actually meet the standard you're holding them to.  
      
    
-   Build a Clear [Exceptions Process  
    ](https://louisville.edu/policies/policy-exception-management-process "This link leaves the DubBlog website.")Document the rationale, assign ownership, and set expiration dates so exceptions don't quietly become the norm.  
      
    
-   Make Quality Part of _Done  
    _Treat accessibility, content quality, SEO, and other standards as requirements in your [definition of complete](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/definition-of-done "This link leaves the DubBlog website.").

**Automated Oversight** 

[Use technology](technology-powering-effective-web-governance.html) to [continuously monitor](../2025/a-guide-to-continuous-accessibility-monitoring.html) for compliance gaps, and equip team members with the knowledge and skills needed to fix issues and build quality checks directly into their workflows.

-   Automate, Where Appropriate  
    [Use automated tools](automated-testing-is-a-rockstar-too.html), like DubBot, to catch accessibility issues, style guide deviations, SEO fundamentals, and other shared standards, and pair it with manual and end-to-end testing for a more complete picture. _Automation should not replace human assessment.  
      
    _
-   Shift from Periodic Audits to Real-time Monitoring  
    [Embed quality checks into your project requirements](accessibility-belongs-in-your-project-requirements.html), content design, development, publishing, and maintenance lifecycle so issues surface in real time.  
      
    
-   Make Performance Visible  
    Dashboards, reports, and scorecards turn abstract standards into metrics so teams can track progress, align priorities, celebrate wins, and stay accountable.  
      
    
-   Prioritize Issues  
    Not every issue carries equal weight. Be explicit about what demands immediate attention versus what can safely wait.

## Enforcing Website Standards Doesn’t Mean You Have to Centralize Control

The goal of web governance isn’t to control every decision; it’s to build standards into the system so good decisions happen consistently. Centralizing standards while decentralizing execution allows organizations to move quickly without fragmenting their brand or compromising accessibility. But only when all three objectives work together. When they do, organizations can scale their digital presence without sacrificing accessibility, brand integrity, content quality, or accountability. Done well, governance gives groups in all disciplines the autonomy to do their best work while giving the organization confidence that the web experience remains consistent, compliant, and sustainable.
