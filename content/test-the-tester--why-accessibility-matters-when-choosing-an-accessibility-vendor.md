Last summer (August 2025), I wrote a blog post ([_When Your Accessibility Tool Isn’t Accessible_](../2025/when-your-accessibility-tool-isnt-accessible.html)) about what to do to prevent purchasing an accessibility tool or platform that itself fails its own accessibility tests.

BTW, the short answer is to _require every vendor to submit an_ [_Accessibility Conformance Report_](https://www.section508.gov/sell/acr/ "This link leaves the DubBlog website.") _(ACR). An ACR is a report card that shows how well a product, like software, a website, or an application, meets accessibility standards like the_ [_Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)_](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/ "This link leaves the DubBlog website.") _or other requirements like_ [_Section 508_](https://www.section508.gov/ "This link leaves the DubBlog website.") _in the U.S._

Now, back to **this** blog post. ☺️

There is another way to evaluate a potential vendor’s product. Their website.

Several days ago, a colleague mentioned a newcomer to the digital accessibility automated testing scene. Through a little digging, another colleague of mine found that this organization has actually been around a while, since 2018. And by the way, something else happened in 2018. January 18, 2018, to be exact. That’s the day the [Section 508 refresh](https://equalentry.com/section-508-refresh-five-things-you-should-know/ "This link leaves the DubBlog website.") went into effect. (Maybe everyone didn’t get the news. Keep reading.)

So, like I always do, I found the company’s website and began to explore. And out of curiosity (actually, this is a habit from way back), I ran a quick accessibility test using [WebAIM’s WAVE browser extension](https://wave.webaim.org/extension/ "This link leaves the DubBlog website.").

Wow, was I surprised. 😯 Not just at the number of accessibility issues, but at the basic skill level required to prevent those issues.

Back in 2016, when I began my accessibility journey, one of my go-to resources was the [7 Core Accessibility Skills](https://accessibility.umn.edu/guides-resources/7-core-accessibility-skills "This link leaves the DubBlog website.") from the [University of Minnesota](https://system.umn.edu/ "This link leaves the DubBlog website."). To this day, I consider this list the most logical and practical introduction to accessibility and a set of compliance criteria that is straightforward to implement, easy to teach, and simple to test.

I reference this list because several of the criteria on it showed up in the WAVE report. Not a good sign for an organization bidding for your trust (and money) in keeping **your** website accessible.

There's a certain irony in an accessibility testing vendor failing accessibility basics on their own website. It doesn't necessarily mean their tool is bad. But it is a reason to ask harder questions, run your own tests, and not take the sales pitch at face value.

So the takeaway here is this. Don't stop at the ACR. Request it, definitely. But also do your own accessibility testing on the vendor's website. Use your accessibility tool of choice, and see what you discover. If an organization selling accessibility has barriers at their own front door, that tells you something an ACR alone won't.
